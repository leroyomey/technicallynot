---
name: tnai-report
description: The "Technically Not report" block every task ends with; Technically Not parses it for results, next actions and ideas.
for: every-agent
tnai-hidden: true
---
- End your final message of each task with this short block (one line each, items separated by "; ", leave out lines with nothing to say): "Technically Not report" on its own line, then "- Did:", "- Found:", "- Changed:" (file names only; say what changed in the reply above the block), "- Decided:", "- Remains:" (real open problems only), "- Untested:" (finished work that has only not been tried for real, e.g. it needs a real CLI update or an installed build to confirm; each item "<what> → try: <how to confirm it>", no fix percentage; anything actually broken or unfinished goes under Remains instead), "- Resolved:" (the keys, like 1a2b3c4d:next:0, of open items in `tnai context` that this work finished, even ones you were not asked about; only when actually done), "- Blockers:" (errors, failed or skipped checks), "- Next:" (only steps someone still has to start; Technically Not offers to send them to you, so never list work you already started or scheduled yourself, such as a background wait that wakes you: put that under "- Remains:" as already scheduled), "- Ideas:" (optional suggestions the user may want, each ending with its fit for this project as "(fit NN%)", rated honestly as in the idea-fit rule; the user can send each one to an agent). Technically Not shows it to the user and the other agents.
- Keep reports easy to read. One full report per task: when a turn only answers a decision, adds to work still in progress, or asks the user something, end it with a follow-up report of only the lines that changed, usually one or two (e.g. "- Did: took Decision 2 (option 1), no other change"), and give the full report once, when the task is done. Messages the user sends while you work are folded into that one report, not reported one by one.
- Only what's new: never repeat items from your own earlier reports in this conversation (they are already in the bell and `tnai context`).
- Short items: at most 3 items per line, each one plain sentence of about 15 words; "- Did:" starts with a summary a non-programmer understands. Keep the fix sentence and "(fix NN%)" on Found / Remains / Blockers items short too.
- Fewer lines: leave out every line with nothing important; most reports need only "- Did:" and "- Changed:" plus one or two others.
- Next and Ideas need a purpose (everything proposed has a purpose, or it is not proposed): each "- Next:" or "- Ideas:" item becomes a one-click task for the user, so write one only when you can say which open question or goal of the user it serves and what result would change a decision. Otherwise leave the line out; most reports have no "- Ideas:" line. When a line of work has answered its question, or two results in a row did not change the picture, say that it is answered and stop; do not propose one more variation.
- Status summaries (only when the user asks what you were doing, where things stand, or you resume or pause work; not in ordinary replies): group the summary under these exact headings, each on its own line with a colon, items as a list under it: "Done:" (finished work), "Where we stopped:" (the current point, one line), "Still open:" (what is left). Leave out a heading with nothing under it. Technically Not colors them in the terminal (done green, stopped yellow, open purple), so keep the words exactly.
- Notices the user must not miss: when a reply holds one thing the user should not skim past and that is not a step for them or a report item (a suggestion to /compact or /clear, a warning that something they rely on changed or will stop working, a limit about to be reached), put it in its own paragraph starting with the exact words "Heads up:", in one or two plain sentences. Technically Not shows that paragraph in amber on a tinted row in the terminal. At most one per reply, and never for ordinary information, or it stops standing out.
- Steps the user must do: whenever a reply tells the user to do something themselves (click, paste, sign in, run a command, look at something and answer), put those steps, and only those, under the exact heading "Your turn:" on its own line, as a numbered list right under it with no blank line between the steps; a single step can follow the heading on the same line. Technically Not colors that section in its own color in the terminal, so the user sees "do this" apart from explanations and findings: keep explanations out of it, and use it in any reply, not only status summaries. "Need your help:" (a step only the user can do, like a sign-in) is colored the same way.
